An AI team is a group of Copus sessions that work on one task too big for one conversation, led by a team lead you talk with. The lead plans the work, starts each teammate's sessions, reads what comes back, and hands you the finished work. You talk to one lead, and it runs the rest of the team, so you don't start or prompt the sessions yourself.

A team can do what a single conversation can't:

- **Keep going for days.** The requirements, the teammates, their memory and the team folder stay from one session to the next. Copus wakes the lead each time a teammate's session ends, so the work moves on without you starting it.
- **Run several sessions side by side.** Up to 20 teammate sessions can work at once, each on its own piece.
- **Check every result.** The team lead reads each piece of work before it reaches you, and a teammate can ask for an independent review of its work.
- **Remember how you want the work done.** Each teammate keeps standing instructions and a memory that every later session of that teammate starts with.
- **Pick up new work on a clock.** A teammate can run on a schedule, such as every hour, and check for new work while you're away.

For a result you want now, start an ordinary task. For the same result again and again, such as a weekly report, make a [scheduled task](/autonomous/autonomous-tasks).

## How a team grows

You build a team as you work with it, and it changes as the work does.

**You start in Collaborate.** A new team is you and the team lead. You describe the task, and the lead asks what it needs to know, then writes the requirements for you to approve: what the task is for, what finished looks like, and the rules everyone on the team follows. In **Collaborate**, the lead works on what you ask for, and each session it wants to start waits for you to select **Start**.

**The team grows with the work.** The lead does the work itself while it can. When part of the work runs side by side for a long time, keeps coming back, or needs to happen on a clock, the lead proposes a teammate for it, and you approve each one. When you state a lasting rule, the lead proposes it for the requirements or for one teammate's standing instructions. When a teammate's work goes wrong in a way that would happen again, the lead proposes a fix to that teammate's standing instructions. Nothing in the requirements or the standing instructions changes until you approve it.

**You switch it to Autonomous.** Once the requirements and each teammate's standing instructions say how you want the work done, turn on **Autonomous**. The lead then starts sessions itself, within the requirements, and scheduled teammates run on their own. You can keep this to set hours, such as overnight. The team asks you under **Needs you** only for what it can't decide or do alone.

## An example: a backlog that turns into branches

This example is an illustration of one way to use a team.

Say you keep a list of changes you want in an app's code, and you want each one designed, reviewed and built on its own branch, ready for you to merge.

1. You start a team and tell the lead about the list and how you like changes designed and built. The lead writes the requirements, such as "every change gets a design you approve before anyone builds it" and "nothing merges until you review it". You approve them.
2. In **Collaborate**, you work through the first few items with the lead. As the list grows, it proposes teammates, such as a Designer and a Developer, each with standing instructions drawn from what you told it. Later it proposes a scheduled teammate that reads the list twice a day for new items.
3. You set the team's **Sandbox level** to **Coding** and approve the build and test commands the team needs, so its sessions can work on code.
4. When the work runs the way you want, you turn on **Autonomous**. The scheduled teammate picks up new items, and designs and finished branches arrive under **Ready**, each read by the lead before it reaches you. When the lead needs your approval to go on, it asks under **Needs you**.
5. You answer questions, approve designs, and merge the branches yourself, as the requirements say. With nobody watching, the team can't push code.

## How the team holds your standard

The team lead owns the quality of what reaches you. It keeps the requirements true as the task moves, reads each teammate's work as you would, and sends back what falls short with what to change. A teammate can also ask for an independent review, which runs as a separate session that checks the work and reports back.

Decisions that matter stay with you. The requirements, every teammate and every change to one, each session in **Collaborate**, and each website, saved secret or command the team may use while you're away wait for you to approve them. When the lead needs a decision it can't make within the requirements, it asks you under **Needs you**.

## Start a team

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create the team">
    In the sidebar, select **AI Teams**, then **+ New team**. You can also ask Copus to set you up with a team, and select **+ New team** on the card it shows. Copus creates a team named "New team" and opens the chat with the team lead.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Describe the task">
    Tell the team lead what you need, in the box that reads "Describe the task…". The lead asks what it needs to know.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Approve the requirements">
    The team lead writes the requirements from your conversation and pins them above the message box, on a card labelled **Requirements · for your approval**. Select **Read the requirements** to open the card in full. Besides the requirements, it lists what you approve with them: **Teammates who join when you approve**, the websites and saved secrets the team may use under **While you're away, the team may use**, any **Hours**, and any commands under **Allowed outside the sandbox once you approve**. To leave a teammate out, select the x beside its name. Select **Approve**, or **Dismiss** to approve nothing. To change anything else, say so in the chat.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Until you approve the requirements, only the lead's tile shows, and you can't turn on **Autonomous**.

To read or edit the requirements later, select **Requirements** at the end of the row of teammates. Editing them yourself counts as approving the new text. If a card from the lead is still waiting when you edit, your edit replaces it: the card closes as "Replaced by your edit", Copus doesn't add the websites, saved secrets or commands it asked for, and each teammate it proposed comes back as a card of its own.

To rename the team, select **Team actions** > **Rename team**.

## Teammates

A teammate is a lasting role on the team, such as Writer or Developer, that does its work in sessions. Only the team lead proposes a teammate. You approve it under **Needs you**: select **Add to team** on a **New teammate** card, or **Approve** on a **Change to a teammate** card.

Select a teammate's tile to open its page, then **Standing instructions and memory** to read what it follows and what it learned. You can edit the instructions and correct the memory there.

A scheduled teammate runs on its schedule only while the team is in **Autonomous**. On its page, select **Run now** to start a run at once. After four failed runs in a row, Copus stops the schedule, and the page shows **Start the schedule again**.

## Collaborate and Autonomous

The **Autonomous** switch in the team's top bar decides how much the team does without you.

- **Off**, the team is in **Collaborate**. Each session the lead wants to start waits under **Needs you** as **Session to start**, with the teammate and the brief, until you select **Start**.
- **On**, the team lead starts sessions itself, within the requirements, and scheduled teammates run on their schedules.

The first time you turn on **Autonomous** on a team with no hours, Copus asks "Run on set hours?". Select **Set hours** to have the team run on its own only at set times, such as overnight or on weekends, and the lead agrees the hours with you in the chat. You approve them on the requirements card. Outside its hours, the team works as in **Collaborate**, and Copus skips a scheduled teammate's runs. To change the hours later, ask the team lead.

When you turn **Autonomous** off, each session that hasn't started yet waits under **Needs you** as **Session to start**.

A team does its own work with nobody watching, so its sessions run on **Autopilot**. With nobody watching, Copus only reads your connected apps, and refuses anything that would need your answer, such as a push, a website it doesn't trust, or a command outside the sandbox that isn't on the team's list. When a teammate needs something it can't do, the lead asks you under **Needs you**. [Approvals and permissions](/control/approvals-and-permissions) has the full rules.

## Work with the team

**Needs you** holds everything the team waits on you for, such as a question from the lead, a teammate or session to approve, or a website the team wants to use. The lead's chat also shows the requirements card, and the lead's questions and requests one at a time, above the message box. When something arrives, Copus sends a **Team needs you** notification. See [Notifications](/working/notifications).

**Ready** holds finished work, with the teammate who built it. Open a piece to read it, select **Give feedback to** the teammate who built it or someone else to talk about it, and select **Mark done** when you're finished with it.

**Updates** shows what teammates post as they work. You can't reply there. To tell the team something, use a chat.

The team opens on its chat with the team lead. Once you approve the requirements, select the pencil, **New conversation with the team lead**, to talk with the lead about something else while its main chat keeps working. A teammate's own sessions take no messages; you can read them on its page, and you talk with a teammate by giving feedback on its finished work.

## Settings and permissions

Select **Settings** on the right of the team screen.

- **Runs on**: the AI provider and model for the team lead and every teammate.
- **Sessions at once**: how many teammate sessions and reviews may work at the same moment, from 1 to 20. A new team starts at 5. The team lead and scheduled runs don't count.
- **Hours**: the team's hours, once it has some. To change them, ask the team lead.
- **Sandbox level** and **Allowed outside the sandbox**: how much of your Mac the team's sessions can read, and the commands they may run outside the sandbox with no card. See [The sandbox](/control/sandbox#work-on-code).

Only you change these settings, in Copus on your Mac.

Select **Permissions**, at the end of the row of teammates, to see the websites and saved secrets the team may use while you're away. You add one only by approving a card from the team, and you take one away with **Remove**, on your Mac.

## Where a team's files live

Each team has a team folder under `CopusData/projects` in your home folder. Every teammate has a folder of its own there, which only that teammate's sessions and your conversations with it can change. Finished work goes in **Artifacts**, on the right of the team screen.

To give the team a folder of your own, such as a code repository, select **Team folder**, then **Add folder**. Copus works on that folder where it is. See [Your data stays local](/control/your-data-stays-local).

## Delete a team

Select **Team actions** > **Delete team**. Every session in the team stops, and Copus deletes the team, its teammates, their conversations and the team folder. Folders you added to the team stay where they are.

## What the team screen shows

A teammate's tile shows the task it's working on, or "2 tasks at once". Otherwise it shows **Blocked**, **Waiting for review**, **Runs on schedule**, **Schedule stopped** or **Idle**. The lead's tile reads **Running the team** while the team runs on its own in **Autonomous**, and **Working with you** otherwise, including outside the team's hours. A red dot marks a reply or an approval waiting in one of your conversations with that teammate.

The cards under **Needs you** are **Team lead asks**, **Website for the team**, **Saved secret for the team**, **Command for the team**, **Requirements ready**, **Change to the requirements**, **New teammate**, **Change to a teammate**, **Session to start**, **Something needs you**, and **Team paused**, which has **Try now** for when the team's AI provider stopped working.

## Limitations

- **Copus works only while it's open.** A team works only while Copus is open and your Mac is awake.
- **The team pauses when its AI provider fails.** When the provider isn't answering or hits its usage limit, the team pauses and Copus keeps trying for a day, then stops. When Copus is signed out of the provider, the team waits and carries on once you sign in again. When the provider is out of credit, Copus keeps trying; once you add credit or save a new key, select **Try now** to carry on at once.
- **Copus makes mistakes.** Read finished work before you use it.
- **You set up a team on your Mac.** You create a team, change its settings, and edit its requirements only on your Mac. See [Copus on your iPhone](/working/iphone) for what the phone can do.
