The message box is where you type what you want Copus to do. There is a message box on the Home page, at the bottom of every task, and in the **Side chat** drawer. Press Enter to send, and Shift+Enter to start a new line. If a message fails to send, Copus puts your text and attachments back in the box.

The box formats markdown as you type. Type `**bold**` and the words turn bold while the asterisks disappear from view, but the asterisks are still there in what Copus receives, because Copus receives the markdown itself. Underscores are the exception. Markdown normally reads a pair of them as emphasis, so Copus's box switches that off and a name like `Q3_final_v2` reaches Copus exactly as you typed it.

This page covers how to:

- [Attach a file to your message](#attach-a-file-to-your-message), by picker, drag, or paste
- [Add a folder Copus can change](#add-a-folder-copus-can-change)
- [Dictate instead of typing](#dictate-instead-of-typing)
- [Add a message while Copus works](#add-a-message-while-copus-works), and steer the work in progress
- [Choose how much the conversation does without asking](#choose-how-much-the-conversation-does-without-asking)
- [Check how full the conversation is](#check-how-full-the-conversation-is)
- [Ask in Side chat](#ask-in-side-chat), without starting a task

## Attach a file to your message

Select the plus button under the box, then **Attach files**, and pick what you want. Dragging files onto the box does the same, and so does pasting an image or a screenshot straight from the clipboard.

Copus shows each attached file as a chip carrying its name, in the same row as the plus button. Select the × on a chip to drop that file before you send. An image you dragged or pasted shows as a thumbnail instead of a name, because Copus already has the picture; an image you picked through **Attach files** shows as a name like any other file.

## Add a folder Copus can change

To have Copus work on files where they already live, such as a code repository or a folder of drafts, select the plus button under the box, then **Add folder**, and choose the folder. Copus asks you to confirm before it gets access. The folder stays with the conversation after you send. [Tasks and the workspace](/concepts/sessions-and-workspace#add-a-folder-copus-can-change) covers what Copus does in an added folder and how to remove it.

## Dictate instead of typing

Select **Voice input**, the microphone button under the box, and speak. While Copus connects to your microphone, the tooltip reads **Connecting to your microphone… start talking when the bars appear**. Your words land in the box as you go, and the microphone icon becomes bars that rise and fall with the sound reaching the microphone, so a muted microphone looks different from a working microphone.

Select **Stop recording** to finish. You can also just select **Send**: Copus waits for the last words to land before it sends them, and the microphone button reads **Finishing…** while you wait.

Type while dictation is running and Copus sets recognition aside rather than ending it. The microphone stays open, everything you already said stays in the box as ordinary text you can edit, and Copus starts listening again when you stop typing.

To talk with Copus out loud instead, select **Talk with Copus**, the waveform button next to **Voice input**. Copus listens, sends what you said when you pause, reads its reply aloud, and listens again until you select **End**.

Copus can also read one of its replies aloud with the microphone off: hover the reply and select **Read aloud**, the speaker under it. It reads a digest the same way. See [Digests](/autonomous/autonomous-tasks#digests).

While Copus listens, for dictation or for voice mode, your Mac turns down other sound that's playing, such as music or a video, and brings it back when Copus stops listening.

Dictation runs on your Mac. Copus transcribes your speech with a speech model it downloads once when the app starts and keeps on the machine, so your audio never leaves it.

<Note>
  **Voice input** stays inactive until that download finishes, and its tooltip reads **Voice input is still being set up**. The button turns on by itself once the download is done.
</Note>

<Warning>
  The first time you dictate, your Mac asks for microphone permission. Decline it and Copus records nothing, and shows **Copus needs permission to use the microphone** under the box. Allow microphone access for Copus on your Mac, then select **Voice input** again.
</Warning>

## Add a message while Copus works

Type while Copus is working and press Enter, and the message waits its turn rather than interrupting. Copus holds it above the box under the heading **Waiting to send**, and the heading counts your messages once you queue a second, as **2 waiting to send**. A waiting message survives closing the app.

Each waiting card carries two controls:

- **Send now** hands that message straight to the work already running. Copus passes it to the AI provider, which picks it up between one step and the next, so nothing is canceled and nothing restarts.
- **Remove**, the × on the card, takes the message back.

Leave the card alone and Copus sends the message on its own when the current turn finishes. If you select **Send now** while a step is still running, Copus holds the message until that step ends, and the card reads **Delivering when the current step finishes** in place of **Send now**. **Remove** stays, so you can still withdraw it.

Use the queue to correct course. Queue the correction, such as "use the Q3 numbers, not Q2", and select **Send now**. Copus changes direction without starting the task over.

<Tip>
  Select **Stop** to halt the current turn outright, rather than queueing a correction behind it. While a turn runs, Copus shows **Stop** in place of **Send**.
</Tip>

## Choose how much the conversation does without asking

Each conversation has an approval mode that sets how much Copus does without asking you. Set it from the dropdown under the message box, whose tooltip reads **Approval mode:** followed by the mode, such as **Approval mode: Manual**. It covers the whole conversation, not one message. In a conversation that always runs on **Autopilot**, such as a scheduled task's own conversation, Copus shows **Autopilot** in place of the dropdown, with the tooltip **This conversation always runs on Autopilot**.

In **Manual**, Copus asks you by default before it sends, posts, or changes anything in a connected app, and before it runs a command that changes something on your Mac. On **Autopilot**, Copus runs commands and makes most changes without asking for the rest of the conversation, and still asks before it sends, posts, deletes, or shares something in a connected app. On **Full access**, Copus runs nearly everything without asking. When you choose **Autopilot** or **Full access**, Copus asks you to confirm, under the heading **Turn on Autopilot for this conversation?** or **Turn on Full access for this conversation?**. Switching back to **Manual** takes effect straight away, with nothing to confirm.

A new task starts in the mode set by **New tasks start in**, which is **Manual** until you change it. Open **Settings**, select **Built-in tools**, and set it there. Copus confirms that choice too. A task in a project you pointed at a folder of your own starts in **Manual** either way, because that work writes into your folder rather than Copus's own. [Approvals and permissions](/control/approvals-and-permissions) covers what each mode allows.

<Warning>
  Neither **Autopilot** nor **Full access** lifts the limits macOS puts on what a task may touch, and neither changes what Copus is refused outright. Two actions ask you every time, however the conversation is set: reading a file that holds sign-in credentials, and working in the Chrome you are signed in to yourself. A scheduled task's runs, a multi-step task's steps, and [work Copus starts on its own](/working/before-you-ask) have nobody to ask. Copus never offers that work the Chrome you are signed in to, and refuses a credential file outright rather than asking.
</Warning>

<Note>
  The approval dropdown appears in a task. On the Home page, send your first message, then set the mode.
</Note>

## Check how full the conversation is

Copus sends the whole conversation to the AI provider on every turn, and the provider can take only so much at once. A ring under the box shows how much of that room the conversation has used, and hovering the ring gives you the figures behind it. The ring turns from green to yellow, orange and red as the conversation fills. To make room, select the ring and then **Compact now**: Copus summarizes the conversation so far and carries on from the summary. Typing `/compact` does the same. Copus also compacts on its own when the conversation is nearly full, and asks first if you switch to a model the conversation is too long for.

When you come back to a long conversation after a break, Copus sends a shorter version of it to save tokens: its first messages, its latest messages and its summary if it has one. Copus reads the rest from the conversation when it needs it. A line in the conversation marks the spot.

The ring appears once a task has a conversation to measure.

## Ask in Side chat

The **Side chat** drawer holds one standing conversation, kept apart from your tasks. Ask it about Copus itself or any other quick question. To open it, select the chat bubble at the bottom of the sidebar, or press `⌘⇧K`. The drawer works on every screen except Settings, once you have set up an AI provider.

In either drawer, Copus answers directly and briefly, without the questions and plan it uses for a larger task. Copus creates a file only when you ask for a file. Each drawer starts on **Autopilot**, and you can switch its mode under its message box like any task. Each message runs on the AI provider that is your default when you send it. Both drawers keep attachments, dictation, and **Stop** from the message box. You can't add a folder in either.

When an answer arrives while a drawer is closed, Copus marks its button with a dot. Neither conversation appears in your **Tasks** list. To start over, select **Clear**. Copus deletes the conversation and its files, and keeps any facts it saved to your global memory. To change the drawer's width, select **Widen the panel** or **Narrow the panel**.
