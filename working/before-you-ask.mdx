Copus reads the apps you connect, finds the work that's waiting on you, and does it before you ask. It produces the documents, analyses, and research people ask you for, writes prep documents for your meetings, drafts replies that need thought, and suggests follow-ups on conversations that have stalled. Each result waits on the Home page until you review it.

This page covers how Copus decides what to take on, what it hands back, and how to review, refine, or stop it. For tasks you start yourself, see [How Copus works](/concepts/how-copus-works).

## How it works

Every 15 minutes, Copus reads what changed in your Gmail and decides which threads ask something of you. For each thread that does, Copus creates a task, does the work, and puts the result on the Home page. You review the result, ask for changes, and decide what happens next.

For example, when a colleague emails to ask for a comparison of two vendors by Friday, Copus might:

1. Read the thread and note the request and the deadline.
2. Suggest the comparison as an idea on the Home page, because the work is large.
3. Research the two vendors after you select **Do it**.
4. Write the comparison as an analysis with charts.
5. Put the analysis on the Home page and notify you.

You read the analysis, ask for a change with **Refine** if something is off, and select **Done** when you finish with it.

Copus follows the same steps for every other kind of result: a document someone requests from you, a prep document for an upcoming meeting, a reply that needs thought, a proposed calendar event, or a set of choices when a decision is yours to make.

Before its first task, Copus builds your People list from your sent mail. While Copus works on its first tasks, it writes a guide to how you write. Every week, Copus updates both from what you do with its results.

## Apps Copus works from

Copus finds work in Gmail. Google Calendar, Google Drive, and your other connected apps give Copus context while it works.

| App | What Copus does with it |
|---|---|
| Gmail | Reads every thread that changes, except mail Gmail files under Promotions, Social, or Forums, and starts or suggests a task when a thread asks something of you |
| Google Calendar | Shows your meetings in the **Coming up** box on the Home page, each with the prep document Copus wrote for it, if any. Also checks each proposed event for clashes |
| Google Drive | Gives Copus access to your files while it works on a task |

## Requirements

- You're signed in to an [AI provider](/concepts/ai-providers).
- Google is connected.
- Copus is open, and your Mac is awake.

## Connect your apps

One Google sign-in connects Gmail, Google Calendar, and Google Drive together. Copus starts reading your mail as soon as you connect. You can connect Google from two places:

- **Connect Google**, during setup or on the Home page.
- The **Google** card in **Settings** > **Integrations**.

Both open the same panel. Tick the agreement box, select **Connect**, and leave every box ticked on Google's sign-in page. If you untick a box, Copus connects none of the three apps.

If you skip Google during setup, you can connect it later from either place.

### What Copus reads first

When you first connect Google, Copus reads your mailbox once to learn about you:

1. Copus reads your recent sent mail and builds your People list. The people you write to most, and most recently, rank highest.
2. Copus reads your recent threads with the people at the top of your People list, and creates its first tasks from them. At the same time, Copus writes a guide to how you write and saves facts about you to your [memory](/context/memory).

To see and edit what Copus learned, see [What Copus learns about you](/context/what-copus-learns).

## What Copus takes on

Copus creates a task when a thread asks for thought or effort from you:

- A reply that needs thought, such as a decision you have to answer with care
- A document, analysis, comparison, or research someone requests
- A conversation where you're waiting on someone, or someone is waiting on you
- A question only you can answer, such as a yes or no, a date, or a preference

Copus skips mail that needs no work from you, such as a reply you'd write in seconds.

Copus starts small, clear tasks right away. When a task is large or its scope is unclear, Copus suggests the task as an idea instead. Copus starts the idea when you select **Do it**, or when new mail on the idea's thread changes the work. Copus also suggests work nobody asked for, such as a summary of where a project stands, but only from conversations with the people at the top of your People list. That kind of work always arrives as an idea.

Once a day, Copus looks for Gmail threads where nobody has replied for three days or more. When someone is waiting on you, Copus starts the reply or suggests it. When you're waiting on someone, Copus usually suggests a short follow-up for you to send. Copus leaves a quiet thread alone when the silence has a reason, such as a settled matter.

When a thread shows that a meeting needs preparation, Copus writes a prep document. Copus shows the document next to the meeting in the **Coming up** box on the Home page.

While a task is open, new mail on its thread goes to that task. When the new message changes what the task needs, Copus works on the task again with the new message in front of it. New mail on the thread of a closed task starts a new task.

## What you get back

When Copus finishes a task, the task on the Home page holds what Copus produced. A task can hold more than one result, such as an analysis and a reply to send with the analysis.

| Result | What Copus hands you |
|---|---|
| Email draft | A reply to the thread in your writing style, with the recipients and subject filled in. Select **Send** to send it, or **Save as draft** to keep it in your Gmail drafts. |
| Analysis | A written answer to the request in the thread, with charts. When the thread needs a response, Copus adds a reply for you to send. |
| Summary | Written findings, such as a synthesis, research or an overview of where something stands. When the thread needs a response, Copus adds a reply for you to send. |
| Meeting prep | A prep document for an upcoming meeting. Copus shows the document next to the meeting in the **Coming up** box on the Home page. |
| Calendar event | A proposed event with the title, time, and guests filled in. Copus lists any meeting already on your calendar at that time. |
| Calendar change | A change to an event you organize, a cancellation, or an answer to an invite. Select **Save changes**, **Cancel event**, or **Send answer**. |
| Drive sharing or comment | Giving someone access to a Drive file, taking access away, or a comment or reply on a file. Select **Share**, **Remove access**, **Post comment**, or **Post reply**. |
| File | A file Copus made for the task, such as a presentation, spreadsheet, PDF, Word document, or image. |
| Question | A decision only you can make, with two or three choices. Each choice holds the reply or event Copus prepared for that answer. |

Two kinds of task wait on you before Copus produces a result:

- **Idea**: Copus suggests the task and hasn't started it. The task shows why Copus suggests it and what you get from it.
- **Stuck**: Copus stopped because it needs something from you. The task names what Copus needs.

Copus notifies you when a result is ready, when a task needs your answer, and when a task is stuck. To change these alerts, see [Notifications](/working/notifications).

## Review and respond

On each result, you select the action that fits it, such as **Send**, **Save as draft**, **Create event**, or **Done**. On an idea, you select **Do it** to start the task or **Decline** to turn it down. Two actions also shape what Copus does next:

- **Refine**: you tell Copus in one line what to change, and Copus reworks the same task.
- **Dismiss**: Copus closes the task and records your reason: **I handled it myself**, **Not something I want Copus to work on**, or **Didn’t do it well**. The weekly review reads these reasons.

To answer a question with something other than the choices offered, select **Something else**, describe what you want, and select **Prepare reply**. To unblock a stuck task, type the missing detail into **Tell Copus what it needs to carry on**, then select **Refine**.

The Home page shows your most urgent tasks first. To see every open task, select the line below the list, such as "Four more", to open the **Copus's tasks** list. To see each step Copus takes on a task, select **Open transcript**.

Your digests have their own box under **Coming up**. See [Digests](/autonomous/autonomous-tasks#digests).

Copus closes a task tied to a time, such as meeting prep, once that time passes.

## How Copus learns from your responses

Copus records what you do with each result: send it unchanged, edit it before sending, refine it, dismiss it, or decline it. Once a week, Copus reviews the week and updates:

- **Your memory**, with facts under **Proactive help** about the work you want and the work you don't
- **Your writing guide**, where your edits show a habit the guide doesn't describe yet
- **Your People list**, with notes on the people in that week's tasks

Copus saves a fact when it sees a pattern across your tasks. You find your writing guide on the **How you write** tab of the **About you** page, next to the **People** and **Memory** tabs. To review or edit what Copus saved, see [What Copus learns about you](/context/what-copus-learns).

## When Copus checks

| Schedule | What Copus does |
|---|---|
| Every 15 minutes | Reads what changed in Gmail since the last check |
| Daily | Looks at quiet Gmail threads, and starts or suggests replies and follow-ups |
| Weekly | Reviews the week's results and updates your memory, writing guide, and People list |

To stop the 15-minute and daily checks for a while, select **Pause checking** on the Home page. See [Stop Copus from starting tasks](#stop-copus-from-starting-tasks).

## Stop Copus from starting tasks

To stop Copus from starting tasks for a while, select **Pause checking** on the Home page. It sits next to the line that says when Copus last checked your mail, such as **Checked 5m ago**. While Copus is paused, it stops the 15-minute mail check, the daily look at quiet threads, and its first read of your mailbox if that read hasn't finished. The line then reads **Checking paused**. Tasks you start, your scheduled tasks and digests, the **Coming up** box, and the weekly review keep running. Select **Resume** to start again. Copus checks your mail as soon as you resume.

To stop Copus for good, open the **Google** card in **Settings** > **Integrations** and select **Disconnect**. Copus then can't use Gmail, Google Calendar, or Google Drive in tasks you start either.

## Limitations

- **Copus finds work in Gmail only.** Google Calendar, Google Drive, and your other connected apps support the tasks Copus starts.
- **Copus skips three Gmail tabs.** Mail that Gmail files under Promotions, Social, or Forums never becomes a task, including mailing-list mail that lands in Forums.
- **Copus works only while it's open.** Checks run only while Copus is open and your Mac is awake. When you open Copus after a missed check, Copus runs that check once. If your Google sign-in expires, the Home page shows a message, and checks stop until you connect Google again in **Settings** > **Integrations**.
- **Copus can misjudge a thread.** Copus sometimes takes on a thread you'd ignore, or misses a thread you want handled. The weekly review reads every task you dismiss or decline.
- **Copus changes nothing in your connected apps on its own.** While Copus works on a task by itself, it only reads your apps. You send every email, create every event, and make every other Google change yourself, with the button on the task, such as **Send**, **Save as draft**, or **Create event**. After you select **Do it**, **Refine**, or **Check in**, or type a message in the task, Copus works as it does on **Autopilot**: it makes ordinary changes, such as editing a Drive file, and asks before it sends, posts, deletes, or shares something.
- **Copus writes only to people it knows.** A reply can go only to someone on a thread Copus read, or to someone in your People list.
- **Your mail goes to your AI provider.** To decide what needs work and to do it, Copus sends mail to your AI provider. The first read sends up to 60 of your most recent sent emails, and up to 40 threads from the last 14 days with the people at the top of your People list. Each 15-minute check sends every changed thread outside the Promotions, Social, and Forums tabs in full, with Copus's notes on the people in it, and contacts your provider only when a thread has changed. The daily check sends the quiet threads it looks at. See [Your data stays local](/control/your-data-stays-local).
