Copus starts work from what it finds in the apps you connect. Copus also runs the tasks you type into the message box on the Home page. Both kinds of task use what Copus knows about you and the apps you connect.

This page covers how both kinds of task run, what Copus reads while it works, and what you control.

## Compare the two ways work starts

| | Work Copus starts | Work you start |
|---|---|---|
| Where it starts | A Gmail thread or a meeting on your Google Calendar | The message box on the Home page |
| How Copus scopes it | Copus reads the thread and decides what the work is. | Copus answers a quick request straight away. For larger work, Copus asks you what it needs to know, then plans the steps. |
| Where the result appears | On the Home page, as a task for you to review | In the conversation, with finished files under **Artifacts** |
| What Copus sends | Copus sends a reply only when you select **Send**, and creates an event only when you select **Create event**. | A new task asks you first. By default, Copus asks before it sends, posts, or changes anything in a connected app, and before it runs a command that changes something on your Mac. You can let a task do these without asking. |

## How Copus starts work on its own

Every 15 minutes, Copus reads what changed in Gmail, reads your upcoming meetings, and starts the work they ask of you. That work includes documents, analyses, research, meeting prep, replies, follow-ups, and decisions laid out as choices. Copus learns from what you do with each result. Each result waits on the Home page for you to review, refine, send, or dismiss.

[Work Copus starts before you ask](/working/before-you-ask) covers what Copus takes on, what it hands back, and how to review or stop it.

## How a task you start runs

A task you start runs in four stages.

**You describe.** Type what you want in the message box on the Home page, and name the outcome rather than the first step. "Pull the Q3 numbers into a one-page summary for Thursday" gets further than "open the spreadsheet." Copus opens a conversation for the task. See [The message box](/working/describing-what-you-need).

**Copus plans.** Copus answers a quick request straight away. For larger work, Copus first asks the questions whose answers change the result, such as the goal, the audience, or the format. You answer them on one card. Copus then posts its steps as a card headed **Execution plan**.

**Copus works.** Copus works through the steps in order. It reads files, searches the web and your documents, uses the tools and apps you have switched on, and writes the output. A strip above the message box names the step running now.

**You review.** Copus replies with charts, sortable tables, diagrams, images, and files that open beside the conversation. Copus writes Word documents, PowerPoint decks, spreadsheets with live formulas, and PDFs, and lists finished files under **Artifacts**. To correct the work, reply in the same conversation. Copus keeps your files, the plan, and your answers from the earlier round.

## What Copus reads while it works

Every task you start reads the following:

- **Memory**: the facts Copus keeps about you between tasks, and the rules Copus follows on every message. Copus builds part of your memory from your mail and from what you do with its work. See [Memory](/context/memory) and [What Copus learns about you](/context/what-copus-learns).
- **Instructions and profile**: your name, your about-me note, and the standing instructions you write for every task, for a project, or for one conversation. See [Instructions and profile](/context/instructions-and-profile).
- **Response style**: the length, tone, formatting, and emoji you set for Copus's replies. See [Response style](/context/response-style).
- **Your documents**: the folders you add as knowledge sources. Copus searches them before it asks you questions. See [Knowledge sources](/context/knowledge-sources).
- **Your apps and the web**: the apps you connect, and the pages Copus reads in its own browser. See [Integrations](/connections/integrations) and [Web and sign-ins](/connections/web-and-sign-ins).
- **Skills**: written instructions for one kind of work, such as building a slide deck. Copus loads the skill that matches the task. See [Skills](/working/skills).

## What the AI provider receives

Copus runs each task on the AI provider you sign in to: Claude Code, Codex, or a model you reach with an API key from Anthropic or OpenAI. See [AI providers](/concepts/ai-providers).

Copus stores your files, projects, knowledge sources, memory, People list, and writing guide on your Mac. Copus sends the AI provider only what a task uses:

- The conversation, and each file or document the task reads
- Your memory facts and standing instructions, when a conversation starts and again when they change
- Your memory rules, with every message
- For work Copus starts on its own, the mail threads Copus reads, with Copus's notes on the people in them

See [Your data stays local](/control/your-data-stays-local).

## Choose how much Copus does without asking

A task you start has an approval mode: **Manual**, **Autopilot**, or **Full access**. You switch the mode in the controls under the message box. In **Manual**, Copus asks you by default before it sends, posts, or changes anything in a connected app, and before it runs a command that changes something on your Mac. On **Autopilot**, Copus runs commands and makes most changes without asking for the rest of the conversation, and still asks before it sends, posts, deletes, or shares something in a connected app. On **Full access**, Copus runs nearly everything without asking. A new task starts in **Manual** unless you change **New tasks start in** under **Settings** > **Built-in tools**.

<Warning>
  On **Autopilot**, Copus can change a record in a connected app without showing it to you first. On **Full access**, Copus can also post a Slack message without asking. Use **Manual** for any task that might post a message or change a record. Email, calendar changes, and Drive sharing and comments are the exception. In every mode, Copus shows you a card for those first, and nothing goes out until you select the button on it.
</Warning>

Copus also starts tasks on its own, from your mail and calendar. In those tasks, Copus changes nothing in your connected apps by itself, and you send every reply and create every event yourself.

In a task you start, Copus always asks before it reads a file that holds credentials or works in your own Chrome, in every approval mode. In work that runs without you, Copus refuses both. [Approvals and permissions](/control/approvals-and-permissions) covers each approval button.

## Hand Copus work that runs on its own

**Multi-step tasks.** For a job too big for one conversation, select **Task actions** in the top bar, then **Switch to multi-step**, or ask Copus to keep at the job until it is done and select **Switch to multi-step** on the card Copus offers. Copus then plans the job, splits it into steps, runs each step in its own conversation, and continues until the job is done. See [Multi-step tasks](/autonomous/multi-step-tasks).

**Scheduled tasks.** For work you want repeated, such as a weekly report, Copus runs the same task on a schedule you set. See [Scheduled tasks](/autonomous/autonomous-tasks).

Copus runs both without asking you, as it does with work it starts from your mail. In both, Copus only reads your connected apps, apart from emailing and inviting the people you approved for the task.

## Limitations

- **Copus works only while it's open.** Copus runs tasks only while it's open and your Mac is awake. A scheduled run or a mail check that comes due while Copus is closed runs once when you open Copus again.
- **Copus makes mistakes.** Read a result before you send it anywhere.
- **Copus starts work from Gmail and Google Calendar only.** Your other connected apps give Copus context for its tasks.
